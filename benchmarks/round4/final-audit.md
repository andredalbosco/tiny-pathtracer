# Astra final audit: round four

No new blocking correctness defect was found in the reviewed final sources. Keeping PCG32 as the default and making xoroshiro128++/128** opt-in matches the evidence. The quality executable defaults to NEE, while historical uniform sampling remains explicitly selectable. This audit is read-only except for this report; no source or benchmark changes were made.

## Provenance and final quality result

The final quality table belongs to binary `87a3befb87285fb4c04ef3dbeafd6563edb47767040a45ec8db97722b622ac2b`, before the optional RNG code was added. I verified that `pre-rng-render_driver.h` exactly reproduces its recorded header hash, `09b3035388fa3c45e628ae8fb31c7abdc1c4f1fd0d97cc0e96534ecb062aa7f9`. The current source hashes separately match `rng/metadata.json`, including header `9e2d50e10ef1c97882acd87a0d78110f361e2e653b51709ea14d22ca00cb510b`. All 24 matched PCG quality cases in the RNG phase—eight seeds each for uniform, cosine and NEE—have the same stored image hashes and MSEs as the corresponding final-quality cases. Their timing observations remain separate. MIS was not one of those three RNG-phase modes.

Use `quality-final`, not the earlier prototype table, for final headlines. At 128 spp and 128×128, the ratio of mean per-run MSE×wall-time products is **10.344× for NEE versus uniform**. It is 11.903× at 32 spp and 9.107× at 512 spp. The secondary `max(reference RGB)<2` mask gives 78.144× at 128 spp and retains 99.353% of pixels. This brightness mask must not be presented as the unqualified full-image result. The mean of paired ratios is a different statistic and should not be substituted under the same label.

The independent 49,152-spp reference, noise-floor estimates, and finite-depth/offset cautions from `astra-results.md` still apply. NEE/MIS error differences remain extremely small for this scene; simpler NEE is a reasonable default, without claiming a universal quality or runtime advantage. The high-sample agreement tests are consistent with the intended common target but do not prove unbiasedness; retain the disclosed uniform brightness difference of 2.311 estimated standard errors.

The final quality scaling rows are explicitly **MIS, 128×128, 512 spp**, despite NEE now being the executable default. Their homogeneous speedups are 7.449× on eight P cores (93.11% of linear) and 15.171× on sixteen E cores (94.82%). Runtime ranges overlap enough that the short three-observation std/OpenMP/visibility comparisons do not justify a universal winner. Do not silently relabel these as NEE scaling results.

## Optional RNG correctness and evidence

The two transitions match their respective authors' algorithms: ++ uses output rotation17 and state rotations49/28 with shift21; ** uses its own output scramble and state rotations24/37 with shift16. All arithmetic is unsigned. Fixed rotation counts avoid zero/64 shift cases. `next()` returns the high32 bits and `uniform()` retains the high24-bit [0,1) conversion. Both mutable words pass through the existing AoS/SoA interface; the saved test asserts the 16-byte Random size.

The master seed is mixed outside the per-path loop via `Random::seed_key` and combined with the full pixel/sample ID before two SplitMix-derived state values are formed. That avoids the raw seed-plus-ID or seed-XOR-ID aliasing mistake. The PCG key function is the identity, preserving its seed semantics. The internal `seed(value,stream)` now expects the prepared key in xoroshiro builds; the renderer call sites satisfy that contract. Consecutive SplitMix inputs are distinct and its bijective finalizer cannot map both to zero. Hash initialization is still not a formal disjoint-subsequence guarantee.

Saved tests cover known answers, uniform bounds/moments, a million-value serial sequence, bins, and 200,000 adjacent-seed/stream pairs. The scheduling checks provide additional deterministic transport coverage. These are implementation checks and statistical smoke tests, **not a completed BigCrush/PractRand validation or a proof of independent streams**.

For the 40-draw short-stream microbenchmark, median ++ cost is 0.318007 ns/draw versus PCG's 0.443800: **28.34% less time**, equivalently about 39.56% greater microbenchmark output rate. Two-draw streams are slower with ++, confirming that setup matters. The harness includes per-path seeding and checksum work, excludes the hoisted master-key calculation, and allows compiler scheduling/vectorization across its regular workload. These numbers are not standalone instruction latency.

Integrated uniform-render medians do not establish an RNG gain: P1 PCG/++/** are 3.6328/3.6828/3.6817 s; all32 are 0.27760/0.27559/0.27873 s with overlapping ranges. E1 comparisons use only 819,200 paths, whereas P1/all32 use 13,107,200, so compare RNGs within each group and do not interpret raw P1/E1 times as scaling. Different random sequences also change live-ray counts. These RNG rows must remain distinct from separately trained PGO intersection experiments.

The eight-seed lower-budget MSE comparison does not establish a general statistical-quality ranking. Separate high-sample NEE mean-image agreement gives observed-difference/noise ratios 0.996 for ++ and 1.199 for **, with brightness differences +0.738 and −1.762 estimated standard errors. This finds no clear mean discrepancy at the tested scale; it neither proves unbiasedness nor warrants replacing PCG. The final default choice is appropriate.

## Perf interpretation

The P-core numerator experiment lowers instructions/path from 4160.61 to 4149.16 (−0.275%) while increasing cycles/path from 1518.14 to 1540.65 (+1.483%). Its dedicated divider-active count falls from 2.85256 billion to 1.17366 billion (−58.86%). Therefore reduced divider activity did not translate into a throughput win. Divider-active cycles are not removable stall cycles; neither their reduction nor the source-level division count is a speedup estimate. Extra dependency/selection work and compiler scheduling are plausible explanations, not separately proven causes. E-side basic counts have only one observation per variant. Hardware measurements remain homogeneous and explicitly PMU-specific, with reported running fractions about 99–100%; unprofiled timing remains the performance score.

The final NEE P-core profile has roughly 2,000 cycle samples and reports no lost samples. Parent verified production/debug `.text` identity (reported hash prefix `6632665`); this audit read the local perf reports, rather than independently re-extracting binary sections. Inclusive shares are visibility 50.36%, ordinary ray-batch intersection 20.16%, cosine-direction generation 11.60%, and total scatter 72.60%. Visibility and cosine generation are nested within scatter, so do not sum all four or label the entire scatter symbol as intersection. These are approximate composition from one sampled run, not exact stage times or guaranteed savings.

The measured next target is bounded shadow visibility: compare batching/reuse against scalar any-hit's ability to terminate early. Camera-jitter stratification remains the strongest simple quality follow-up because a tiny bright-pixel subset dominates NEE's global error. Preserve independent estimator validation across more depths and scenes before combining altered floating-point intersection kernels with the quality modes. Neither optional RNG replacement nor additional division removal currently has a demonstrated integrated benefit to justify changing the default.
